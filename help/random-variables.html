Random Variables

The sims whose formulas use randomness have a Random variables box. Each line of it names one random variable and says what it is: u ~ unif(0, 1) is a uniform number in [0, 1] called u, and X = 2 u + 1 is a random variable made from it. The sim's own formulas then use the names. The chart under the box shows the distribution function F(x) = P(X ≤ x) of any of them.

Try it

How a line reads

LineWhat it says
u ~ unif(0, 1)u has a law from the list below, here the uniform law on [0, 1]. The parameters can be numbers, letters (each gets a slider), or formulas of them, like normal(0, 2 s).
w ~ {x >= 0: e^(-x), 0}w has this density, a formula in x, written as in Desmos: {condition: value, ...} is a piecewise formula, and the last value, with no condition, is "otherwise".
w ~ {x = 0: 1/2, x > 0: e^(-x)/2}A piece whose condition is an equation is a point mass: here w is 0 half the time, and otherwise exponential. A condition like mod(x, 1) = 0 puts a mass at every whole number where it holds. The masses and the density must add up to 1, or the box says what they add up to and refuses the line.
X = max(u, v)X is a formula of other random variables (and sliders): any formula the sims take, like {c = 1: u, v}, abs(u) or floor(6 u) + 1.
d_k ~ unif([0, 2])A whole family: d1, d2, … are independent, each 0 or 2 with chance 1/2. Square brackets are a list.
X = sum(k, 1, 40, d_k / 3^k)The sum of dk/3k for k = 1, 2, …, 40 (here, nearly the Cantor law). prod(k, a, b, ...) is the product the same way. The ends are whole numbers, at most 10,000 terms apart.

Names

  • A name is one letter, Latin or Greek, maybe with digits after it: u, u1, β2. It can also have a subscript after an underscore: e_5 is a name of its own, and d_k, with a letter as its subscript, is a family. A family is used as d_3, or as d_k or d_(k + 1) inside a sum or a product over k.
  • Names that formulas already know, like e, i and pi, can't be random variables, and neither can x (the density's letter) or the letters a sim uses itself (each sim's box says which).
  • Any other letter in a line is a slider. A family's law can't depend on its own index, and the lines can't go round in a circle (X = Y and Y = X).

The menu next to the chart puts in any law from the list with its starting parameters, for the name being charted, or one of a few formulas of random variables (random digits, a Bernoulli convolution, Weierstrass's function at a uniform point, a perpetuity, a mixture, the larger of two).

Where the numbers come from

Each time a sim works out its formulas somewhere, a name is one number, the same everywhere it appears: in the matrix [[u, 1 - u], [1 - u, u]] both u's are the same number. Each place where the sim works its formulas out gets new, independent copies of every name (and of every dk):

SimIts namesA place is
First Passage Percolationw, the passage timean edge (or a cell, with the times on the cells)
Sinusoid-ish g, the gap between two pointsa gap
Matrix Products any, used in the matricesa step of the product
Lattice Path Counting any, used in X, Y and the definitionsa point (x, y), and each new input of a definition like d(y)

Each copy is F−1(U), where F is its law and U is a uniform number fixed by the seed, the name, its index and the place. F−1(U) has the law F (the "inverse transform" [1, Theorem 1.2.2]), and F−1(U) only goes up as U does. So the sliders are coupled: moving a slider in u's law moves every u a little, all the same way, and leaves the other names alone, and the same seed always gives the same numbers.

How the chart is worked out

The line under the chart says which of these it is.

  • F from its formula. The law's own F. Where the law has a formula for F−1 too, like the exponential law's −log(1 − u)/λ, the sim uses it; otherwise F−1 is read off a fine table of F.
  • F added up from its density. The density is added up on a fine grid, for the laws whose F has no formula (and for typed densities).
  • F worked out from draws. For a formula of random variables, the chart is the F of many draws of it (up to 16,384, fewer if the formula is slow, so the chart takes about half a second), spread evenly over the uniform numbers by a Latin hypercube [2]. That is only for the chart: the sim works the formula out exactly at each place. A few laws in the list need more than one uniform number for each draw (stable laws, Bernoulli convolutions); their charts come from 20,000 draws, and the sim's own draws are exact.

Every law

Each law is typed as its name with its parameters, like normal(0, 1). Unless a row gives its own source, the formulas are the standard ones in [3] (continuous laws) and [4] (discrete laws).

Checks

The check page tests every law in the list (its F against its density or masses, F−1 against F, its mean and variance against their formulas), pairs of laws that are the same law made two ways, and the random variables themselves: one number per name in each place, coupled sliders, independent names, and random series.
