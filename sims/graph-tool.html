    About the tool

    Paint a domain on the grid. The painted cells, joined to their painted neighbours, form a graph; that graph is what a simulation will use.

    Painting. With Paint chosen, drag to paint in the current colour. Starting a drag on a cell that already has that colour erases instead, so a click turns a cell on or off. The Eraser always erases. Swap colour changes the chosen square's colour (cells already painted with it change too), and + Add colour adds a new one. Move drags the view; the mouse wheel or a pinch zooms at any time.

    Coordinates. Cell (x, y) is the unit square centred at the point (x, y): it covers [x−½, x+½] × [y−½, y+½]. In the dots view, the same vertex is the dot at (x, y). As in Desmos, the black lines are the axes, numbered every 5 units. The light grey lines are every unit: the cell borders in the cells view, and through the dots in the dots view, where darker grey lines also mark every 5 units.

    Neighbours. With 4, two cells are joined when they share a side: this is the square lattice ℤ2. With 8, cells that share only a corner are joined too (the “king's graph”). Since each cell is centred on a lattice point, “cells sharing a side” and “dots one step apart” give exactly the same graph, so the cells and dots views are two pictures of one graph.

    Torus. With wrap-around on, the right edge is joined to the left and the top to the bottom, giving (ℤ/Wℤ) × (ℤ/Hℤ), where W and H are the grid's width and height in cells. Copies of the drawing, with their own axes and grid lines, repeat in every direction however far you pan. Numbers on the axes are the real coordinates, so each copy is labelled the same. Painting a copy paints the real cell.

    Formula. Type a condition such as x^2 + y^2 <= r^2 or 4 <= x^2 + y^2 <= 25; each cell whose centre (x, y) satisfies it is painted, and the grid follows the formula live as you type or move a slider. Every letter besides x and y gets a slider. As in Desmos, xy means x·y and = means equals; use abs(x) for |x|, and and, or, not to combine conditions. Replace shows only the region; Add, Remove and Keep only combine it with what's already drawn. Done (or painting) keeps the result as one Undo step.

    Colours. Each colour reaches a simulation as its number (1, 2, 3, …); 0 means off.

    Files. Save file and Copy as JSON keep everything. Copy as edge list gives one edge per line, like 3,-2 4,-2, which other programs read easily (for example networkx's read_edgelist), but it forgets colours and any painted cell with no painted neighbour.
