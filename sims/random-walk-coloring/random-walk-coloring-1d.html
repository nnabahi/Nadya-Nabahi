About the model

N random walkers wander along a row of cells, each with its own color. Every cell keeps the color of the first walker to land on it, and keeps it for good. The run ends when every cell is colored.

Each step, a walker moves one cell left or right, chosen at random. On the ring the two ends are neighbors, so it is drawn as a circle; on the line a walker at an end can only step back in. In discrete time all walkers step together, and if several land on a new cell at once, one of them is picked at random to color it. In continuous time each walker has its own random clock, and only one walker moves at a time.

In 1D each color is a single arc (or interval) at first, and the interface is the set of places where two colors meet.

Things to try: drag the walkers to new starting places, then press Play; switch between the two models; change N or the length.

The domain is the cycle C_L (ring) or the path P_L (line) on L cells. Walker i, for i = 1, \dots, N, is a simple random walk X^i_t: each step goes to a uniformly chosen neighbor (on the line, an end cell has only one). The color of cell v is the i whose walker reaches v first. The walkers start evenly spaced, walker 1 on the top cell of the ring (the left end of the line).

Background. Two walkers competing on a cycle are studied in [1]; see also [2] for two walkers on a one-dimensional lattice.

References
