1. Is every packing equally likely?

Each test is a tiny domain whose packings can all be listed. The chain runs for many moves, and every few moves the page notes which packing it is in. One bar per packing shows how often it was seen, divided by how often it should be seen if all were equally likely, so a uniform chain gives bars all near the line at 1. Pearson's chi-square test (K. Pearson, Phil. Mag. 50 (1900) 157) sums up how uneven the bars are: p is the chance of bars at least this uneven if the chain were uniform. Below 0.001 counts as "NOT uniform". Notes taken only a few moves apart are alike, which makes p too small, so leave enough moves between notes; a short run can also flag a correct chain by chance.

2. Does the search find every tiling?

The chain's refills come from a search that must find every tiling exactly once. Domino tilings of an m × n box are counted exactly by Kasteleyn's formula (P. W. Kasteleyn, Physica 27 (1961) 1209; H. N. V. Temperley & M. E. Fisher, Phil. Mag. 6 (1961) 1061), so the search's counts are compared with it. The 2 × n row is the Fibonacci numbers.

3. The arctic circle

A uniformly random domino tiling of an Aztec diamond is "frozen" (all dominoes lined up the same way) in the four corners outside the inscribed circle, and mixed inside it (W. Jockusch, J. Propp & P. Shor, "Random domino tilings and the arctic circle theorem", 1998, arXiv:math/9801068). Dominoes are colored by direction and by which checkerboard color their lower-left cell has (4 colors). "Average" mixes the colors by how long each cell has spent in each, since the last reset. Let it run, press "Reset average" once the picture has settled, and a circle should appear.
