Internal Continuous DLA: Check Page

Each particle carries a tile out from the source by Brownian motion and stops the first moment its tile overlaps no tile of the cluster: the moment the point leaves the union U of the copies of D = T − T around the tiles. The sim does this exactly, except that a particle stops once the nearest free point is within a tiny epsilon, and moves straight to it. The first checks use cases whose answers are known exactly: from inside one disk (ball) Brownian motion leaves it with the law of the Poisson kernel, and from the middle of a square (cube) it leaves each eighth of a side (each of 48 pieces of the cube's faces) equally often. The others test each piece, in the plane and in space: how deep a point is in a copy of D, the nearest free point against a plain search, the ball around the source that U is known to hold, the jumps against Brownian motion in small steps, what epsilon changes, a big cluster recounted (every tile touches an earlier one, and none overlap), coupling, the outline of D for tiles with dents (like the star) against the gap between two copies found another way, and that the 3D tiles it can't do yet say so.
