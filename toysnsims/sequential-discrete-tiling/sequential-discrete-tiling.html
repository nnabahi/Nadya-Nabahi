About the model

(The simple explanation is coming.)

The model. The domain D is a finite set of cells of the square grid \mathbb{Z}^2, and the tiles are k \times k squares of cells. For n = 1, 2, 3, \dots, while some cell of D is uncovered:

  1. U_n is an uncovered cell, all of them equally likely;
  2. k_n is the largest k such that some k \times k square of uncovered cells of D contains U_n;
  3. tile n is one of the k_n \times k_n squares of uncovered cells that contain U_n, all of them equally likely.

The 1 \times 1 square \{U_n\} always fits, so every step places a tile, and after at most |D| steps D is covered. It is the grid's version of the Sequential Continuous Tiling, where a uniform point grows the biggest tile it can. Clicking an uncovered cell makes it the next U_n.

The domain. By default D is a polyomino with N cells, from the chain of the Uniform Polyomino sim (see its All the details), started from a straight line and with that sim's options: up to K cells moved at once, and the heats b and s, which tilt the odds of a polyomino P by e^{b L(P) + s T(P)}, with L(P) its edge perimeter and T(P) its site perimeter. With both heats 0, after enough moves every polyomino with N cells is close to equally likely. It can also be a box, or any region drawn in the graph tool (in one piece or several, but not on a torus).

Finding the squares. A square of uncovered cells that contains U contains the cells of U's row inside it, so its side is at most the length r of the run of uncovered cells through U along its row, and likewise along its column. Let K be the smaller of the two. Every square that matters then lies within K - 1 cells of U in each direction, and in that window the page works out, for every cell (a, b), the side L(a, b) of the biggest square of uncovered cells with (a, b) as its lowest, leftmost cell:

L(a, b) = 1 + \min\big(L(a + 1, b),\ L(a, b + 1),\ L(a + 1, b + 1)\big)

on an uncovered cell, and 0 on a covered one or outside D: the square of side \ell at (a, b) is free exactly when (a, b) is, and so are the three squares of side \ell - 1 at (a + 1, b), (a, b + 1) and (a + 1, b + 1), which together cover the rest of it. The square of side k at (a, b) contains U = (u, v) exactly when a \le u, b \le v and k \ge \max(u - a, v - b) + 1, and is free exactly when k \le L(a, b). So k_n and its squares are read off the window directly, in about 4K^2 steps. The model check compares this with trying every square.

Betti numbers. Think of each tile as a closed square, so two tiles that touch only at a corner are joined there. The covered set C then has two Betti numbers: \beta_0, its number of connected pieces, and \beta_1, its number of holes, the groups of uncovered cells (or cells outside D), joined across sides, that C cuts off from far away [1, Theorem 3.44]. The page keeps \beta_0 by joining each new tile to the tiles in the ring of cells around it (union-find [2]), and gets \beta_1 from the Euler characteristic,

\beta_0 - \beta_1 = \chi(C) = (\text{corners}) - (\text{sides}) + (\text{cells})

[1, Theorem 2.44], counting each corner and each side of the covered cells once. As tiles are placed, pieces are born and merge, and holes open (when tiles close a ring around uncovered cells) and later fill in. When D is fully covered, C = D, so the run ends at D's own Betti numbers.

Random numbers. Tile n draws U_n and its square from two numbers of its own, made from the seed and n, so the same seed gives the same packing. The polyomino draws its numbers from the seed too, but on their own, so the domain and its packing are independent.
